title: "Writing Cold Emails"

So, you need to reach out to:
- sponsors
- founders
- engineers
- potential mentors
- partner organizations
And you don’t have a warm relationship with that person? Well let’s write them a cold email! These emails will be the first interaction someone has with Purdue Hackers, so writing them well matters.
The goal of a cold email is not to convince someone of everything immediately. The goal is to start a conversation.
Good cold emails are short, clear, credible, and easy to respond to.
## What a Cold Email Needs to Do
Someone opening a cold email is trying to answer three questions quickly:
1. Who is this?
2. Why are they emailing me?
3. What do they want?
If the email doesn’t answer those questions within a few seconds, it will probably be ignored.
Because of this, good cold emails are usually 80-140 words.
## An Example
Consider the following email:
```plain text
Hi Nik,

Hope you're doing well! I'm Artus, a junior studying Electrical Engineering at Purdue.. Also a very happy Revolut user!

I help run Purdue Hackers, the largest CS student organization at Purdue. We support hundreds of student programmers, engineers, artists, and hobbyists each year with the resources and community to build ambitious projects.

I'm currently looking for folks who might be interested in supporting our community going into the next academic year. We really like companies that support people building ambitious things, and Revolut seems to care about that too.

Would you happen to be the right person to talk to about sponsorships or partnerships at Revolut? If not, I’d really appreciate being pointed in the right direction.

Best,
Artus Mosquet
```
This email is not particularly clever. It doesn’t try to persuade aggressively or present a detailed pitch. Instead, it succeeds by answering the reader’s three questions quickly and asking for a small next step.
