## Why?
A *structured introduction to Purdue Hackers* and a funnel to bring people to Hack Night.
## What.
Interactive sessions split in two halves: **guided coding** and **individual hacking**. Guided coding typically means participants manually typing in the presenter’s code, and then individual hacking is self-directed modifications of the built project. Participants should #ship their finished projects at the end, providing an end-to-end idea of what a project done at Purdue Hackers looks like.
> Come and build something fun, and leave having made it your own.
## When!
Every two weeks in a semester. Usually Thursday (day before Hack Night, people have spare time.) Usually between the times of 6:30pm and 8:30pm.
## How?
The following needs to happen for a workshop to happen:
1. Book an appropriate room. Prefer larger rooms. Start from looking at LWSN and WALC.
2. Two weeks before (optional): submit an APF.
3. Have a designer create a poster.
4. One week before: do marketing.
	1. Send emails from phackers (credentials in Vaultwarden):
		1. Professors
		2. CS Opportunity Newsletter 
		3. Data Mine
	2. Send Discord announcement.
	3. Make Instagram post (credentials in Vaultwarden.)
5. A few days before: ping organizers and get a list of who will be able to attend the workshop.
6. Day of the workshop: arrive a few minutes before the workshop, pull up Purdue Hackers intro slides.
	1. **Introduce yourself, then the club**. Ensure that participants feel comfortable interacting with each other and you. Show [these callout slides](https://www.figma.com/deck/xuUkcLvIBTp3hXhOwhyyQs) at the start. PDF attached in case Figma dislikes you.

## How do I make a good workshop?
1. The guided portion should not be too long; a good guideline is **thirty to forty-five minutes at most**. (Remember that guidelines are just that — guidelines!)
2. *Workshops are not tutorials: *Focus on what participants will make, not the tools they'll use. Workshops like "intro to Rust" or "intro to \<tool/language/etc\>" are uninspired. For example, "Sound Galaxy" could be called "intro to creative coding/p5," but it moves from ‘build cool thing’ to ‘learn a thing’.
3. Participants should have *choice* in their hacking: if you have more than three ideas for what participants could accomplish during the hacking section, that means the idea’s really good!
4. Minimize the start friction for participants; prefer web IDEs like Github Codespaces to reduce set up overhead.
